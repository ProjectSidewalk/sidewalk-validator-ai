## Why

Production holds back every Disagree map: a validator that can only Agree cannot remove wrong labels, and the error
class this umbrella is about (real but degraded features that the crowd votes wrong) is exactly where the crowd
reference is weakest. The pieces that would let a validator vote Disagree responsibly (confident learning, the
stricter vote rules, the `majority2` prior, the expert gold set and the VLM adjudicator) were scattered over four
studies, none wired to the others, and nothing said in advance what result would let a Disagree map leave hold-back.

Every model so far trained on the gold set's own labels **and on the rest of their scenes** (other train rows on the
same panorama or within the split's 3 m merge). This run makes the gold set usable as a judge: it trains every
candidate without them, scores every candidate on them, and writes the bar down before anything is scored.

This run is the **"round-4 recipe on round-3 data"**: it reuses round 3's splits, crops and confident-learning flags.
It is not a new data pull (J1).

### Decision rules

**Recipe per type.** `cl-drop` is the recipe for CurbRamp, Crosswalk, NoCurbRamp and Obstacle unless any of:

- **C1 (harm on gold):** P4's interval lies wholly below 0.
- **C2 (in-distribution cost):** its mean test AUC over three seeds is more than 0.010 under the baseline's.
- **C3 (Agree non-regression; a harm screen, amended 2026-11-20, J15):** under `majority2`, with each run's own
  uncapped map fit on val, on `test` or on `test_heldout`: the 95% interval of `cl-drop` minus the gold-free
  baseline in the Agree verdict's prior-weighted coverage at served accuracy 0.92 lies wholly below -0.03 (absolute),
  **or** the 95% interval of the same difference in prior-weighted Agree precision lies wholly below -0.01. The
  statistic is the mean over the shared training seeds of the paired per-seed difference (seed s against seed s).
  The interval is a paired hierarchical bootstrap (2,000 replicates, bootstrap seed 0, as for the other contrasts):
  each replicate resamples the shared training seeds with replacement, a drawn seed bringing both arms' runs of that
  seed, then resamples whole panoramas once, the one resample shared by every drawn seed and both arms, and takes the
  mean over the drawn seeds of `cl-drop` minus baseline; each run's map stays the one fit on val (never refit inside
  the bootstrap). 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. A replicate whose value is undefined (an arm gates no Agree in that
  resample, so its precision is undefined) is dropped and the drops are counted; a clause is unestimable when fewer
  than half the requested replicates remain. One exception: when the precision clause is unestimable because the
  **baseline** gates no Agree on **every** shared seed (its precision undefined on each) while `cl-drop`'s precision
  is defined on every shared seed, the clause reads as a pass, and the bar prints a note saying why (there is no
  baseline Agree precision to regress from); C3 on that split is then judged by the coverage clause alone. When the
  baseline gates no Agree on only some seeds, the clause stays unestimable, because the remaining seeds can carry a
  real precision loss (say 1.000 against 0.870 on seeds 42 and 43, with the baseline undefined on 44); the bar
  records the cause per seed. An undefined `cl-drop` precision, or a mix of the two causes, also stays unestimable
  with the cause recorded. Each
  clause is judged on its own (the 2026-11-19 fix below): a split fails when either clause's interval can be
  computed and lies wholly beyond its threshold (an interval touching the threshold passes), passes when both can
  be computed and neither does, and is unestimable otherwise.

  **What this screen is, with three seeds.** It is a screen, not a calibrated 95% interval. With three seeds a
  replicate draws the same seed three times with probability 1/27, more than 2.5%, so the interval's upper end sits
  at about the best seed's delta. A clause therefore fails roughly when every shared seed loses more than the margin
  (panorama noise moves this slightly either way). If each seed's delta is equally likely to fall either side of
  the margin, all three fall beyond it with probability (1/2)^3, so the one-sided false-fail level at the margin is
  about 12.5%, not 2.5%. With two shared seeds the same reasoning gives a false-fail rate of about 25%, and with
  one seed the interval resamples panoramas only; the `maps` and `bar` blocks print the number of shared seeds
  beside C3 and a descriptive warning when it is under three (a warning, not a floor). A false fail keeps the
  baseline recipe, which is the safe direction. A second reviewer
  simulated the operating characteristics on a simplified model (per-seed delta normal, panorama standard error
  0.012, 4,000 simulations per cell, **a 0.02 margin**; this doc's coverage margin is 0.03, so the rates are
  indicative only, and they are a simulation, not the real data). Each cell is the share of simulations in which
  the clause fails:

  | paired seed sd | true drop | this screen | t-interval, df = 2 | registered rule (mean beyond margin) |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 0.08 | 0 | 0.05 | 0.01 | 0.33 |
  | 0.08 | -0.10 | 0.59 | 0.17 | 0.95 |
  | 0.08 | -0.25 | 1.00 | 0.71 | 1.00 |
  | 0.14 | 0 | 0.08 | 0.02 | 0.41 |
  | 0.14 | -0.10 | 0.39 | 0.09 | 0.85 |
  | 0.14 | -0.25 | 0.88 | 0.37 | 1.00 |

  Beside C3 the `maps` and `bar` blocks print descriptive marks that do not decide it: the **collapse flag**
  (per split, any seed where one arm gates no Agree while the other gates some, or whose Agree coverage delta is
  below -0.09, three times the margin; recorded in `maps.json` and `bar.json`), **"point estimate beyond the
  margin, interval spans it"** for a clause whose seed mean is past its margin while the clause passes,
  **"defined-seed means differ by more than the margin"** when some seeds' precision is undefined and the mean
  precision delta over the seeds where both arms are defined is below -0.01, and the fewer-than-three-seeds
  warning above. The seed
  means, the per-seed values, and whether each arm's Agree precision is under 0.92 are printed as descriptive
  columns too; they do not decide C3 (the last because the map is fit on val and both arms scatter around 0.92 on
  test). `maps` also records the labels missing from some seed or arm (`n_dropped_rows`, left out of the contrast
  with a warning) and refuses a test set whose true class differs between runs.

  **Amendment record (2026-11-20, J15).** As registered, C3 compared bare three-seed means with the 0.03 and 0.01
  margins. October's preview below shows a seed-to-seed spread of 0.03 to 0.44 in Agree coverage at the gate, so
  that rule mostly measured seed noise (in the simulation above it fails 33 to 41% of the time when there is no
  difference). At Jon's direction to follow standard practice in ML experiments (intervals that include seed
  variance; harm screens in the same form as C1 and C4), C3 was amended to the form above on 2026-11-20: after the
  October preview, which is disclosed below, and before any pilot `maps` or `decide` output was produced or read.
  The hierarchical bootstrap over runs and test items is in the spirit of hierarchical-bootstrap practice for
  studies with few training runs (Agarwal et al. 2021, arXiv:2108.13264; Bouthillier et al. 2021,
  arXiv:2103.03098); neither paper prescribes this exact two-level scheme. The same day, after a review pointed
  out that a percentile bootstrap over three seeds is not a calibrated interval, the standard t-interval with the
  seeds as replicates (k - 1 = 2 degrees of freedom, t = 4.30) was considered, briefly committed (b826893), and
  rejected and reverted before any pilot output: in the simulation it catches a true 10-point coverage drop only 9
  to 17% of the time. The bootstrap of 9ca4921 was kept, described above for what it is. The same amendment added
  the baseline-side reading of an undefined precision, the collapse flag and the point-beyond-margin mark. A final
  review the same day found that the baseline-side reading, as first written, passed the precision clause when the
  baseline gated no Agree on only *some* seeds, which would hide a loss on the others; also before any pilot
  output, it was restricted to the baseline gating no Agree on every shared seed (above), and the defined-seed
